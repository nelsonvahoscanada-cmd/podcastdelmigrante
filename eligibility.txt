Claiming asylum in Canada

Who can apply

You can’t claim asylum if you have an active removal order 
(opens in a new tab)
.

Before you start an asylum claim, check if it might be ineligible.

What makes an asylum claim ineligible

Your asylum claim is ineligible if you:

already have protected person status in Canada
have ever made an asylum claim in Canada that was rejected, ineligible, withdrawn or abandoned
have already made an asylum claim in a country that shares information with us
can return to a country where you’re a Convention refugee
are not allowed into Canada (inadmissible) for any of these reasons  
(opens in a new tab)
:
security
human or international rights violations
organized crime
serious crime
enter Canada from the United States (US) along the land border
make your claim more than one year after you first entered Canada after June 24, 2020—even if you’ve left and returned since then Some exceptions apply
make your claim more than 14 days after you entered Canada in between ports of entry along the Canada–US land border Some exceptions apply
If you enter Canada from the US
Exceptions to rules introduced on March 26, 2026
What happens to ineligible claims

Ineligible claims are sent to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for next steps.

Learn more about ineligible claims.

Show that you meet the definition of a refugee

Eligible claims get sent to the Immigration and Refugee Board (IRB), which schedules a hearing to decide if you need Canada’s protection.

During the hearing, you will need to prove that you are physically in Canada and that you meet the definition of a Convention refugee or a person in need of protection.

A Convention refugee

If you’re a Convention refugee, it means you fear returning to your country because you will be persecuted for one of these reasons:

race
religion
nationality
political opinion
being part of a particular social group
A person in need of protection

If you are a person in need of protection, it means you fear returning to your country because:

you’re in danger of torture
there’s a risk to your life
there’s a risk of cruel and unusual treatment or punishment

Also, as a Convention refugee or person in need of protection:

you have to prove authorities in your country can’t or won’t protect you
you may have to show you can’t safely live in another part of your country
