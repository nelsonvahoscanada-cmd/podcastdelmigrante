 Overview
 Kinship care is when a relative or someone with a close relationship to a child or youth cares for them (temporarily or permanently) when their parent(s) cannot. It offers children and youth a chance to grow and thrive in a familiar environment.
\r\n Anyone with a close relationship to a child or youth can provide kinship care including but not limited to:
\r\n \r\n grandparents
\r\n aunts and uncles
\r\n siblings
\r\n godparents
\r\n friends
\r\n teachers
\r\n neighbours
\r\n coaches

 Kinship care is when a relative or someone with a close relationship to a child or youth cares for them (temporarily or permanently) when their parent(s) cannot. It offers children and youth a chance to grow and thrive in a familiar environment.

 Your role
 Kinship care can be both fulfilling and challenging. Children and youth need kinship caregivers who:
\r\n \r\n understand their unique needs and worries about living with another family
\r\n provide stability and consistency in their lives
\r\n create a safe, nurturing, supportive and loving environment
\r\n help them stay connected to their family and support the potential return to their family home (if it is in their best interest)
\r\n help them get involved in the community and explore things they like such as art, sports, music, etc.
\r\n support and respect their routine, culture, traditions, language, religion and identity
\r\n advocate for them and support their best interests, views and preferences
\r\n help them develop skills for the greatest opportunities as adults
\r\n will work as valued member of a support team

 help them develop skills for the greatest opportunities as adults
 will work as valued member of a support team
 Who qualifies to give kinship care
 Anyone with a close relationship to a child or youth may be eligible to provide kinship care. You don’t have to be married, wealthy or have a specific educational background. The top priority is to find children and youth a home where they feel safe, loved and connected to someone they already know and trust.
\n You may be eligible to provide kinship care to a child or youth if you:
\n \n are 19 years or older
\n are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident
\n have a living space that provides a secure and healthy environment for children and youth
\n \n As part of the application process, a social worker will come to your home to check if it’s a safe and welcoming place.

 Anyone with a close relationship to a child or youth may be eligible to provide kinship care. You don’t have to be married, wealthy or have a specific educational background. The top priority is to find children and youth a home where they feel safe, loved and connected to someone they already know and trust.
 You may be eligible to provide kinship care to a child or youth if you:
 are 19 years or older
 are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident
 have a living space that provides a secure and healthy environment for children and youth
 As part of the application process, a social worker will come to your home to check if it’s a safe and welcoming place.
 How to apply
 Call the Department of Social Development at 1-833-733-7835 to speak with a child protection social worker. To complete your application, you must be willing to:
\r\n \r\n authorize a Social Development record check
\r\n participate in a family assessment
\r\n provide all the supporting documents required by the department at any time in the process
\r\n provide a Criminal and Vulnerable Sector Record Check (for all adults 19 years of age or older living in the household, and any immediate family members with a criminal history)
