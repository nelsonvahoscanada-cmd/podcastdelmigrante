 Overview
 Vaccination&nbsp;isn’t&nbsp;just for children. Vaccines protect you and those around you from vaccine preventable diseases.
\r\n As we get older, the protection we had from&nbsp;previous&nbsp;vaccination for certain diseases can decrease. Getting another dose (called a booster) increases&nbsp;our immunity.&nbsp;Some adults may have missed one or more vaccines in childhood and need to receive these vaccines now.&nbsp;There are also diseases that are more common in adults, even healthy adults.&nbsp;By keeping up to date with vaccines, adults not only protect themselves but also prevent the spread of disease.
\r\n Consult the&nbsp; New Brunswick Immunization Schedule &nbsp;and talk to your&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider&nbsp;to find out what regular vaccines you may need&nbsp;to be fully protected.
\r\n Your&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider may ask about your medical history, including:
\r\n \r\n what vaccines you had in the past
\r\n if&nbsp;you're&nbsp;pregnant or plan to become pregnant
\r\n if you have&nbsp;allergies or&nbsp;health conditions
\r\n your type of work
\r\n if you have upcoming travel
\r\n \r\n If you need information&nbsp;regarding&nbsp;vaccination or wish to book an appointment, consult your family doctor, nurse&nbsp;practitioner&nbsp;or a participating pharmacist.

 As we get older, the protection we had from previous vaccination for certain diseases can decrease. Getting another dose (called a booster) increases our immunity. Some adults may have missed one or more vaccines in childhood and need to receive these vaccines now. There are also diseases that are more common in adults, even healthy adults. By keeping up to date with vaccines, adults not only protect themselves but also prevent the spread of disease.
 Consult the New Brunswick Immunization Schedule and talk to your health-care provider to find out what regular vaccines you may need to be fully protected.
 Your health-care provider may ask about your medical history, including:
 what vaccines you had in the past
 if you're pregnant or plan to become pregnant
 if you have allergies or health conditions
 your type of work
 if you have upcoming travel
 If you need information regarding vaccination or wish to book an appointment, consult your family doctor, nurse practitioner or a participating pharmacist.

 Vaccination and pregnancy
 Vaccination is important for everyone of reproductive age, whether planning a pregnancy or not.
\r\n During pregnancy, your immune system&nbsp;changes&nbsp;and this can put you at risk for serious infectious diseases and complications. Your baby can also be affected by these infections, which can result in:
\r\n \r\n birth defects
\r\n premature birth
\r\n miscarriage (losing a baby before birth)
\r\n death
\r\n \r\n Since an unplanned pregnancy can happen,&nbsp;it's&nbsp;important to keep your vaccinations up to date. This will protect you and your baby from certain infections that could cause serious complications. Talk to your&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider about your vaccination status.
\r\n Types of vaccines recommended for pregnant people
\r\n \r\n Tetanus, diphtheria, and pertussis (whooping cough)&nbsp;(Tdap)
\r\n Influenza
\r\n COVID-19
\r\n \r\n Depending on your risk factors, your&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider may recommend other vaccines.
\r\n You should also make sure everyone in your household is up to date with their vaccines. Since babies’ immune systems are not mature, they can become seriously ill if exposed to disease.&nbsp;Newborns are also too young to receive most vaccines, so the best way to protect them is for those around them to provide protection.

 Vaccination is important for everyone of reproductive age, whether planning a pregnancy or not.
 During pregnancy, your immune system changes and this can put you at risk for serious infectious diseases and complications. Your baby can also be affected by these infections, which can result in:
 birth defects
 premature birth
 miscarriage (losing a baby before birth)
 death
 Since an unplanned pregnancy can happen, it's important to keep your vaccinations up to date. This will protect you and your baby from certain infections that could cause serious complications. Talk to your health-care provider about your vaccination status.
 Types of vaccines recommended for pregnant people
 Tetanus, diphtheria, and pertussis (whooping cough) (Tdap)
 Influenza
 COVID-19
 Depending on your risk factors, your health-care provider may recommend other vaccines.
 You should also make sure everyone in your household is up to date with their vaccines. Since babies’ immune systems are not mature, they can become seriously ill if exposed to disease. Newborns are also too young to receive most vaccines, so the best way to protect them is for those around them to provide protection.
