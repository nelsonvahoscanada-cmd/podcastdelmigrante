Tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis (Tdap) vaccine
 The Tdap vaccine protects against three diseases: tetanus (lockjaw),&nbsp;diphtheria&nbsp;and pertussis (whooping cough).

 The Tdap vaccine protects against three diseases: tetanus (lockjaw), diphtheria and pertussis (whooping cough).

 About the vaccine
 The Tdap vaccine is provided&nbsp;free&nbsp;as part of the&nbsp;routine&nbsp; New Brunswick Immunization Schedule &nbsp;for adolescents in grade 7, pregnant individuals and adults. This vaccine boosts the immune system to give better protection against tetanus,&nbsp;diphtheria&nbsp;and pertussis to those who were immunized in early childhood.
\r\n The Tdap vaccine&nbsp;is given as an injection into the upper arm.&nbsp;
\r\n Multiple vaccines can be administered at the same time. This means earlier protection against multiple diseases and fewer appointments. Receiving multiple vaccines at the same time is safe and does not compromise the effectiveness of each vaccine.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
\r\n Adolescents receive the vaccine in grade 7. One dose of Tdap is recommended for adults, given in place of a regular Td (tetanus and diphtheria) booster.&nbsp;The Tdap vaccine should be offered to individuals during every pregnancy.&nbsp;
\r\n It is important for children, adolescents, those who are pregnant&nbsp;and&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;workers in close contact with infants and newborns to be vaccinated.&nbsp;This helps&nbsp;prevent&nbsp;pertussis illness&nbsp;in infants&nbsp;who are too young to be vaccinated.&nbsp;It is safe for breastfeeding individuals to receive the vaccine.&nbsp;

 The Tdap vaccine is provided free as part of the routine New Brunswick Immunization Schedule for adolescents in grade 7, pregnant individuals and adults. This vaccine boosts the immune system to give better protection against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis to those who were immunized in early childhood.
 The Tdap vaccine is given as an injection into the upper arm. 
 Multiple vaccines can be administered at the same time. This means earlier protection against multiple diseases and fewer appointments. Receiving multiple vaccines at the same time is safe and does not compromise the effectiveness of each vaccine. 
 Adolescents receive the vaccine in grade 7. One dose of Tdap is recommended for adults, given in place of a regular Td (tetanus and diphtheria) booster. The Tdap vaccine should be offered to individuals during every pregnancy. 
 It is important for children, adolescents, those who are pregnant and health-care workers in close contact with infants and newborns to be vaccinated. This helps prevent pertussis illness in infants who are too young to be vaccinated. It is safe for breastfeeding individuals to receive the vaccine.

 Benefits of getting the vaccine
 The Tdap vaccine is the best way to protect against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis. These diseases can cause serious illness and complications, including death, especially in newborns and young infants not yet fully immunized. When you and your child are immunized, you also help protect others.

 The Tdap vaccine is the best way to protect against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis. These diseases can cause serious illness and complications, including death, especially in newborns and young infants not yet fully immunized. When you and your child are immunized, you also help protect others.
 Individuals who should not receive the vaccine
 The vaccine should not be given if you&nbsp;or your&nbsp;child had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past or is allergic to any of the ingredients of the vaccine or its container. 
\r\n"}}" id="text-49e6b00f1d" class="cmp-text">
 The vaccine should not be given if you or your child had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past or is allergic to any of the ingredients of the vaccine or its container. 
 Vaccine reactions and side effects
 Inform your immunization provider if&nbsp;you or&nbsp;your child has any health problems or allergies.
\n Reactions&nbsp;to the vaccine&nbsp;are&nbsp;generally mild&nbsp;and last one to two days. The most common reactions include soreness, redness and/or swelling where the vaccine was given.&nbsp;Fever may also occur. &nbsp;For mild reactions, a cold compress at the injection site may reduce discomfort.  Ask a&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider what medicine is best to reduce fever.
\n Less common reactions can include fatigue, headache, nausea, vomiting, joint and muscle&nbsp;pain&nbsp;and rash.
\n With any vaccine there is an extremely rare possibility (less than one in a million people) of a life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving&nbsp;the&nbsp;vaccine.&nbsp;Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat,&nbsp;tongue&nbsp;or lips. This reaction can be treated.  Seek immediate medical attention if you have left the vaccination site and develop serious symptoms described. 
\n If you or your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact your primary care provider, pharmacy,&nbsp;Public Health office&nbsp;or Tele-Care (811).

 Booking a vaccine appointment
 In New Brunswick, the vaccine is offered to all grade 7 students as part the&nbsp;New Brunswick Routine Immunization Schedule. Adolescents who did not receive it in grade 7 and are still in school can contact&nbsp;their local&nbsp;Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité )&nbsp;or primary care provider&nbsp;to&nbsp;book an appointment.
\n Adults&nbsp;as well as pregnant individuals can receive&nbsp;the&nbsp;vaccine through their primary care provider or&nbsp; local pharmacy .

 In New Brunswick, the vaccine is offered to all grade 7 students as part the New Brunswick Routine Immunization Schedule. Adolescents who did not receive it in grade 7 and are still in school can contact their local Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité ) or primary care provider to book an appointment.
 Adults as well as pregnant individuals can receive the vaccine through their primary care provider or local pharmacy .
 Records
 Vaccination records for people aged 16 and older are available by registering online at MyHealthNB . To access your immunization record, please register or sign in to your account.
\r\n The paper record of immunization provided when vaccinated will continue to be the official record. Keep your immunization record in a safe place.
\r\n If the information on your record is incorrect or you received immunizations outside of New Brunswick and want them added, send an email to immunizationrecordupdates@gnb.ca .
\r\n Immunizations administered prior to 1991 may not be readily accessible in MyHealthNB .  If your vaccination record is not available at MyHealthNB , contact the place you received your vaccines.
