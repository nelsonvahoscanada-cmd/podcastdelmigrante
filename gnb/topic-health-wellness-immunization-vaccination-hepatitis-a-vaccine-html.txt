 About the vaccine
 The hepatitis A vaccine provides protection against the&nbsp;hepatitis A virus. The vaccine is approved for individuals&nbsp;six&nbsp;months of age and older.&nbsp;
\r\n The vaccine is given as an injection into the upper arm in older children and adults and into the thigh in infants.&nbsp;Hepatis A vaccine may be given alone or in a combined Hepatitis A&nbsp;and&nbsp;Hepatitis B vaccine.&nbsp;
\r\n The New Brunswick Immunization Schedule&nbsp;provides&nbsp;the&nbsp;hepatitis A vaccine to individuals who have been exposed to the virus through close contact&nbsp;with&nbsp;someone infected. It is also available to those with specific health conditions or circumstances that place them at greater risk of hepatitis A infection.&nbsp;&nbsp;
\r\n Individuals who do not qualify to receive the publicly funded vaccine but still wish to receive it for other specific reasons,&nbsp;such as&nbsp;traveling&nbsp;abroad,&nbsp;may get it at a cost.&nbsp;

 The hepatitis A vaccine provides protection against the hepatitis A virus. The vaccine is approved for individuals six months of age and older. 
 The vaccine is given as an injection into the upper arm in older children and adults and into the thigh in infants. Hepatis A vaccine may be given alone or in a combined Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B vaccine. 
 The New Brunswick Immunization Schedule provides the hepatitis A vaccine to individuals who have been exposed to the virus through close contact with someone infected. It is also available to those with specific health conditions or circumstances that place them at greater risk of hepatitis A infection. 
 Individuals who do not qualify to receive the publicly funded vaccine but still wish to receive it for other specific reasons, such as traveling abroad, may get it at a cost.

 Benefits of getting the vaccine
 The hepatitis A vaccine is the best way to protect against hepatitis A infection.
\r\n While developing countries have the highest prevalence&nbsp;of hepatitis A, the virus is present worldwide, including in New Brunswick.
\r\n It is typically transmitted through the feces of infected individuals and can also be found in blood. Since the vaccine was introduced in Canada in 1996, the&nbsp;occurrence of&nbsp;hepatitis A has significantly declined.
\r\n"}}" id="text-6f09bbe035" class="cmp-text">
 The hepatitis A vaccine is the best way to protect against hepatitis A infection.
 While developing countries have the highest prevalence of hepatitis A, the virus is present worldwide, including in New Brunswick.
 It is typically transmitted through the feces of infected individuals and can also be found in blood. Since the vaccine was introduced in Canada in 1996, the occurrence of hepatitis A has significantly declined.
 Individuals who should not receive the vaccine
 If you&nbsp;or your child&nbsp;had a&nbsp;severe allergic&nbsp;reaction to&nbsp;a previous&nbsp;dose or to any ingredient&nbsp;in&nbsp;the vaccine or its container, speak with&nbsp;a&nbsp;health-care provider.

 Vaccine reactions and side effects
 Inform your immunization provider if you or your child has any health problems or allergies.
\n The hepatitis A vaccine is well tolerated. Reactions are&nbsp;generally mild&nbsp;and limited to soreness and redness at the&nbsp;injection&nbsp;site. Other less common reactions include&nbsp;headache, irritability, fever,&nbsp;fatigue&nbsp;and stomach upset.&nbsp;For mild reactions, a cold compress at the injection site may reduce discomfort.  Ask a&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider what medicine is best to reduce fever.
\n With any vaccine, there is an extremely rare possibility (less than one in a million people) of a life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving the vaccine. Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat,&nbsp;tongue&nbsp;or lips. This reaction can be treated. &nbsp; Seek immediate medical attention if you have left the vaccination site and develop serious symptoms. 
\n If&nbsp;you or&nbsp;your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact&nbsp;a&nbsp;primary care provider,&nbsp;pharmacy,&nbsp;Public Health office&nbsp;or Tele-Care (811).

 Booking a vaccine appointment
 If you have been identified as a close contact of&nbsp;a&nbsp;person infected&nbsp;with&nbsp;hepatitis A and need to be vaccinated, your local&nbsp;Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité )&nbsp;will provide the vaccine.&nbsp;
\n Other individuals who qualify for the vaccine can receive it at their primary care provider,&nbsp;such as a family doctor or nurse practitioner.&nbsp;
\n Those who would like the vaccine for travel purposes can receive it at a cost&nbsp;from a&nbsp;travel clinic or local pharmacy.
\n"}}" id="text-9decc95c52" class="cmp-text">
 If you have been identified as a close contact of a person infected with hepatitis A and need to be vaccinated, your local Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité ) will provide the vaccine. 
 Other individuals who qualify for the vaccine can receive it at their primary care provider, such as a family doctor or nurse practitioner. 
 Those who would like the vaccine for travel purposes can receive it at a cost from a travel clinic or local pharmacy.
 Records
 Vaccination records for people aged 16 and older are available by registering online at MyHealthNB . To access your immunization record, please register or sign&nbsp;in to&nbsp;your account.
\r\n The paper record of immunization provided when vaccinated will continue to be the official record. Keep your immunization record in a safe place.
\r\n If the information on your record is incorrect or you received immunizations outside of New Brunswick and want them added, send an email to immunizationrecordupdates@gnb.ca .
\r\n Immunizations administered prior to 1991 may not be readily accessible in&nbsp; MyHealthNB .  If your vaccination record is not available at&nbsp; MyHealthNB , contact the place you received your vaccines.
