Measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine
 The MMR vaccine protects against three diseases: measles, mumps and rubella (German measles).

 About the vaccine
 The measles, mumps, and rubella vaccine (MMR) vaccine is provided free as part of the routine New Brunswick Immunization Schedule for children and adults. The vaccine is given as an injection into the upper arm.
\r\n Multiple vaccines can be administered at the same time. This means earlier&nbsp;protection against multiple diseases and fewer appointments. Receiving multiple vaccines at the same time does not compromise the effectiveness of each vaccine.

 For children
 The first dose of the MMR vaccine is usually administered at 12 months of age and the second at 18 months.
\r\n Children six to 11 months old travelling domestically to areas with measles activity or internationally (i.e., any international travel, whether or not measles activity has been identified) can receive one dose of MMR vaccine. The early dose does not replace the routine childhood immunization schedule. Two doses of measles, mumps, rubella and varicella (MMRV) will still be required at 12 and 18 months.
\r\n Children born in 2009 and later are now receiving the measles, mumps, rubella and varicella vaccine (MMRV) instead of MMR. The vaccine is given at the same time as other routine childhood immunizations.

 For adults
 Adults born in 1970 or later who have not previously received two doses of MMR (at 12 months old or later) are eligible to receive two doses of the vaccine (one month apart) or receive one dose they previously missed in childhood.
\r\n Most adults born before 1970 are presumed to have immunity to measles from past infection. However, since the risk of being exposed to measles is higher when travelling outside of Canada, we recommend travelers consider getting one dose of the MMR vaccine (if they have never received one before) to make sure they are well protected. 
\r\n This dose is not currently publicly funded. Contact a local pharmacy to inquire about the availability of the vaccine for travelers.

 Increased risk for pregnant individuals
 Pregnant individuals are at a higher risk for complications from measles compared to the general population. Measles infection during pregnancy can lead to serious health outcomes, including congenital measles.&nbsp;
\r\n Although rare, congenital cases of measles have been reported in Canada. Congenital measles occurs when a measles infection passes from pregnant individual to fetus.&nbsp;
\r\n Because vaccines like MMR are not recommended during pregnancy, it is critical to ensure your MMR vaccines are up to date before getting pregnant . Individuals planning pregnancy should confirm their vaccination status and receive the MMR vaccine at least one month before becoming pregnant if they are not already immune.&nbsp;

Individuals planning pregnancy should confirm their vaccination status and receive the MMR vaccine at least one month before becoming pregnant if they are not already immune. 
 Benefits of getting the vaccine
 The MMR vaccine is the best way to protect against measles, mumps and rubella. These diseases can cause serious illness and complications, including death. Ensuring that you and your child are fully immunized also helps protect those around you.

 Individuals who should not receive the vaccine
 The vaccine should not be given if you or your child:&nbsp;
\r\n \r\n had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past&nbsp;
\r\n are allergic to any of the ingredients of the vaccine or its container, including gelatin or neomycin&nbsp;
\r\n are pregnant or planning to become pregnant less than one month of receiving the vaccine&nbsp;&nbsp;
\r\n \r\n Speak with your health-care provider if you or your child has a weakened immune system or had a blood transfusion or received other blood products within the last three to 11 months, as vaccination may need to be delayed.

 had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past 
 are allergic to any of the ingredients of the vaccine or its container, including gelatin or neomycin 
 are pregnant or planning to become pregnant less than one month of receiving the vaccine 
 Speak with your health-care provider if you or your child has a weakened immune system or had a blood transfusion or received other blood products within the last three to 11 months, as vaccination may need to be delayed.
 Vaccine reactions and side effects
 Inform your immunization provider if you or your child has any health problems or allergies.&nbsp;
\n The most common reactions include soreness, redness and/or swelling where the vaccine was given. For mild reactions, a cold compress at the injection site may reduce discomfort. Ask a&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider what medicine is best to reduce fever.
\n Some people may experience less common reactions a week to 23 days after getting the vaccine. Some children may feel tired and have a fever, sometimes with a rash, for up to 3 days. Occasionally, they might also get swelling near the jaw (parotitis), a rash, swollen lymph nodes or joint pain.&nbsp;
\n With any vaccine there is an extremely rare possibility (less than one in a million people) of a life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving a vaccine. Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat, tongue, or lips. This reaction can be treated.  Seek immediate medical attention if you have left the vaccination site and develop serious symptoms. 
\n If you or your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact your primary care provider, pharmacy,&nbsp;Public Health office&nbsp;or Tele-Care (811).

 Records
 Vaccination records for people aged 16 and older are available by registering online at MyHealthNB . To access your immunization record, please register or sign in to your account.
\r\n The paper record of immunization provided when vaccinated will continue to be the official record. Keep your immunization record in a safe place.
\r\n If the information on your record is incorrect or you received immunizations outside of New Brunswick and want them added, send an email to immunizationrecordupdates@gnb.ca .
\r\n Immunizations administered prior to 1991 may not be readily accessible in MyHealthNB .  If your vaccination record is not available at MyHealthNB , contact the place you received your vaccines.
