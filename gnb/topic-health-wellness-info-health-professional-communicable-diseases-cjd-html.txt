 Overview

 Symptoms
 Early symptoms of CJD include anxiety, depression, withdrawal and other behavioral changes, progressing to forgetfulness and other memory impairment, with dementia in the late stages.&nbsp;
\r\n"}}" id="text-fba3b8cb81" class="cmp-text">
 Early symptoms of CJD include anxiety, depression, withdrawal and other behavioral changes, progressing to forgetfulness and other memory impairment, with dementia in the late stages. 
 How it spreads
 The only known cases where CJD has spread from one person to another are a few rare cases of disease transmission caused by injection of human growth hormone or transplantation of human tissue. These medically-related means of transmission have now been reduced.&nbsp;
\r\n"}}" id="text-b0bb2bc7ea" class="cmp-text">
 The only known cases where CJD has spread from one person to another are a few rare cases of disease transmission caused by injection of human growth hormone or transplantation of human tissue. These medically-related means of transmission have now been reduced. 
 Risk factors
 Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease occurs in both men and women all over the world, in about one person per million population.&nbsp;
\r\n"}}" id="text-f6ff962b04" class="cmp-text">
 Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease occurs in both men and women all over the world, in about one person per million population. 
 Treatment 
 There is currently no treatment or cure for CJD.
\r\n"}}" id="text-cc37f92d63" class="cmp-text">
 There is currently no treatment or cure for CJD.
 Prevention
 Most cases of CJD seem to occur with no means of explanation and therefore cannot be prevented. People who have been in close contact with someone who has CJD are not at risk of the disease.&nbsp;
