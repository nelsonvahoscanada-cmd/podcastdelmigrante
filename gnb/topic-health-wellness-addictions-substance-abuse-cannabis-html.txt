 Cannabis is a product of the cannabis sativa plant used for its psychoactive effects. It comes in many forms, including dried flowers and leaves, hash, extracts (such as oil and shatter) and edibles (candies, butter or baked goods).
 Contrary to popular belief, people can become addicted to cannabis. Frequent and heavy cannabis use can cause physical dependence and addiction. 
 Tolerance is characterized by a need for larger doses of a drug to maintain the same effect. Tolerance can develop after only a few doses. In some people, tolerance eventually leads to physical dependence and/or addiction.
 Tolerance and dependence can develop at any age, but young people are especially vulnerable because their brains are still developing.
 The legal age to purchase, possess, cultivate or consume cannabis is 19.  Cannabis NB  stores and Cannabis NB approved retailers are the only legal retailers.
 Health risks
 Cannabis affects the same biological system in the brain responsible for brain development. Young people are more likely to experience harm from cannabis because their brains develop until about age 25. The earlier a person starts consuming cannabis, the more harm it can do.
\r\n The physical effects of cannabis may include:
\r\n \r\n irritated respiratory system from smoking (sore throat, hoarse voice, coughing, wheezing, tightness in chest)
\r\n increase in appetite and heart rate
\r\n decrease in blood pressure, balance and stability
\r\n drowsiness or restlessness, depending on the amount taken and a person’s individual response
\r\n \r\n Consuming too much cannabis at one time or accidentally consuming cannabis can lead to over-intoxication or cannabis poisoning. 
\r\n These long-term health effects are possible for anyone who uses cannabis:
\r\n \r\n impairment, which can lead to serious injuries or death, including those resulting from car accidents and operating equipment 
\r\n problems with physical co-ordination and reaction time
\r\n hallucinations, such as seeing, hearing, tasting, smelling or feeling things that do not really exist 
\r\n changes in mood and feelings, such as fear or panic
\r\n developing or worsening mental health problems – depression, anxiety disorders, suicidal behaviours and psychosis – including triggering a psychotic episode&nbsp;
\r\n interference with cognitive development and long-lasting damage to the brain, especially if cannabis use starts at an early age (under 25 years) before the brain has fully developed 
\r\n breathing and lung problems from smoking cannabis (bronchitis, lung infections, long-term cough, increased mucus buildup in the chest)
\r\n cancer
\r\n \r\n Using cannabis with other substances, including alcohol, opioids, cocaine and prescribed or over-the-counter medications can lead to increased risk of harm.
\r\n There is no known safe amount of cannabis use during pregnancy or breastfeeding. The effects of cannabis can be passed on to a baby during pregnancy or while breastfeeding. Some effects can last the child’s lifetime. The compounds and toxins in cannabis are passed through blood to the unborn baby and through breast milk.
\r\n Smoking cannabis while pregnant is not recommended due to potential risks to both the baby and the mother. Health experts, including the Centres for Disease Control and Prevention&nbsp; &nbsp;and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, advise against cannabis use during pregnancy.
