 Overview
 Hantavirus is disease that is caused by several different strains of the viruses found throughout the world. Depending on the type of hantavirus, two types of illness can occur that affect either the kidneys (haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome) or the lungs (hantavirus pulmonary syndrome). Hantavirus infections in humans are severe but fortunately rare in Canada.
\r\n Hantavirus is diagnosed by the types of symptoms present, a history of exposure to the virus and laboratory testing.

 Symptoms
 Symptoms of hantavirus (depending on the type of virus strains) include either fever, varying degrees of bleeding and kidney involvement (haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome) or a flu-like illness that progresses rapidly to severe lung infection requiring hospitalization (pulmonary syndrome).

 Symptoms of hantavirus (depending on the type of virus strains) include either fever, varying degrees of bleeding and kidney involvement (haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome) or a flu-like illness that progresses rapidly to severe lung infection requiring hospitalization (pulmonary syndrome).
 How it spreads
 In Canada, hantavirus is found only in rodents, including deer mice. Rodents shed the virus in their urine and feces. People become infected by breathing in air or coming in contact with environments that are contaminated with the virus.

 In Canada, hantavirus is found only in rodents, including deer mice. Rodents shed the virus in their urine and feces. People become infected by breathing in air or coming in contact with environments that are contaminated with the virus.
 Risk factors
 People living or travelling in areas of the world where hantavirus is present have a low risk of being infected. In Canada, exposure to rodents and their droppings may increase the risk.&nbsp;

 Treatment 
 Most people with hantavirus will go to the hospital for supportive care. Anti-viral drugs may be used.&nbsp;
\r\n Health-care providers and laboratories must report cases of hantavirus to Public Health. Public Health staff will interview the health-care provider and patient (or caregivers) to find out how the infection occurred, identify other people at risk of infection, implement control measures and provide other advice.

 Most people with hantavirus will go to the hospital for supportive care. Anti-viral drugs may be used. 
 Health-care providers and laboratories must report cases of hantavirus to Public Health. Public Health staff will interview the health-care provider and patient (or caregivers) to find out how the infection occurred, identify other people at risk of infection, implement control measures and provide other advice.
 Prevention
 Follow basic precautions when handling animals . Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water after using the toilet, after handling animals, after handling raw meats and before preparing or eating food.&nbsp;
\r\n Control rodents in areas where hantavirus is present to prevent the spread to people . Prevent rodents from entering and remaining in homes and other buildings by doing the following: seal openings that might let rodents in, use rodent traps, store food and garbage in containers with tight fitting lids and stack woodpiles away from buildings.&nbsp;
\r\n Safely clean rodent-infested buildings . If any rodents or their droppings are seen in a building, open doors and windows for at least 30 minutes to air out the building. Before proceeding to safely clean and disinfect the contaminated areas, put on proper safety equipment (disposable gloves and an N95 rated mask). Do not dry sweep or vacuum as this can increase your risk of breathing in the virus.&nbsp;
\r\n Wet contaminated areas with a solution of one part water to nine parts chlorine and allow to sit for five to 10 minutes. Spray dead rodents with disinfectant before removal. Mop floors and clean any surfaces with the prepared disinfectant. Place any dead rodents in a garbage bad and double bag along with all cleaning materials. Dispose of material by burial, burning or throwing out in appropriate waste disposal system.&nbsp;
\r\n For additional information, contact your health-care provider, local Public Health office or Tele-Care 811 .

 Safely clean rodent-infested buildings . If any rodents or their droppings are seen in a building, open doors and windows for at least 30 minutes to air out the building. Before proceeding to safely clean and disinfect the contaminated areas, put on proper safety equipment (disposable gloves and an N95 rated mask). Do not dry sweep or vacuum as this can increase your risk of breathing in the virus. 
 Wet contaminated areas with a solution of one part water to nine parts chlorine and allow to sit for five to 10 minutes. Spray dead rodents with disinfectant before removal. Mop floors and clean any surfaces with the prepared disinfectant. Place any dead rodents in a garbage bad and double bag along with all cleaning materials. Dispose of material by burial, burning or throwing out in appropriate waste disposal system. 
 For additional information, contact your health-care provider, local Public Health office or Tele-Care 811 .
