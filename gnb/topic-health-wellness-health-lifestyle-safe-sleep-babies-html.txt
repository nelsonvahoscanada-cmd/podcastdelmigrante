 Overview
 Good sleep is important for your baby’s health. A sleeping baby is a peaceful sight and in the first few months, parents spend a lot of time learning about their baby’s sleep habits.

 Good sleep is important for your baby’s health. A sleeping baby is a peaceful sight and in the first few months, parents spend a lot of time learning about their baby’s sleep habits.
 We often think about when and how babies sleep—but where they sleep is just as important.
 Why safe sleep matters
 Not every place is safe for a baby to sleep. Babies under six months are at higher risk because they can’t move their heads or shift their bodies well yet. Sadly, babies can accidentally die while sleeping if they can’t breathe because something is blocking their nose or mouth. This is called suffocation or smothering and can be prevented. This is different from Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS), which is when a baby dies during sleep for no known reason.
 Sleep accidents can happen to anyone. But by choosing a safe sleep space every time, you can help protect your baby.

 Unsafe sleep spaces
 To protect your baby,&nbsp;avoid putting them to sleep in the following locations:

\n car seat
\n chair
\n adult bed (alone or with others)

 couch or sofa
 car seat
 chair
 adult bed (alone or with others)
 These places increase the risk of accidents. Babies can roll into soft pillows, get entangled in blankets or straps, or even be accidentally rolled on by others.
 Tips for safe sleep
 To reduce the risk of SIDS and accidental death related to suffocation or smothering, follow these tips:
\n \n Place&nbsp;the baby&nbsp;on their back to sleep, for naps and at night.
\n Room&nbsp;share. Keep&nbsp;the baby&nbsp;in your room, not your bed. Put their crib or bassinet next to your bed for easy feeding and comfort. This is recommended for the baby’s first six months.
\n Never put your baby to sleep on a couch, sofa, or armchair&nbsp;or&nbsp;to&nbsp;share those surfaces with another sleeping person. If&nbsp;the baby&nbsp;falls asleep on these surfaces, place them in their own bed as soon as possible.
\n Never let baby sleep in car seats, swings, or bouncers for&nbsp;long periods, especially when not supervised.
\n Use a crib, cradle, or bassinet that meets Canadian safety standards, with a firm mattress and a fitted sheet.
\n Keep the sleep space free&nbsp;of pillows, toys, bumper pads, and loose blankets.
\n Prevent your baby from overheating. Dress baby in a sleep sack or onesie instead of using a blanket.
\n Keep your environment smoke free&nbsp;during pregnancy and after&nbsp;the baby&nbsp;is born.
\n Breastfeeding helps protect your baby&nbsp;from&nbsp;Sudden Infant Death Syndrome&nbsp;(SIDS). The longer you breastfeed, the more it helps keep your baby safe.
\n Return&nbsp;the baby&nbsp;to their own bed after breastfeeding and skin-to-skin.

 Never let baby sleep in car seats, swings, or bouncers for long periods, especially when not supervised.
 Use a crib, cradle, or bassinet that meets Canadian safety standards, with a firm mattress and a fitted sheet.

 Return the baby to their own bed after breastfeeding and skin-to-skin.
 Understanding room sharing vs. bed sharing
 Babies should only sleep in places made just for them, like a crib,&nbsp;cradle&nbsp;or bassinet.&nbsp;Bed sharing&nbsp;refers to your baby sleeping in the same bed as adults, other&nbsp;children,&nbsp;or pets. This is unsafe&nbsp;and there is&nbsp;a high risk&nbsp;for accidents&nbsp;because
\n \n babies on adult beds can fall or get trapped between the mattress and walls or furniture
\n blankets,&nbsp;pillows&nbsp;or duvets can increase the risk of suffocation
\n bed sharing is even riskier if adults in the bed are under the influence of alcohol, drugs or medication that causes drowsiness
\n \n Why room sharing is safer
\n Room sharing&nbsp;allows your baby to sleep near you, but in their own bassinet,&nbsp;cradle&nbsp;or crib. This setup is&nbsp;the safest&nbsp;for the first six months of life.
\n Many parents bring their baby into their bed for feeding or skin-to-skin contact, which is fine&nbsp;if&nbsp;the parent is awake and alert. Once feeding or snuggling is complete, place your baby&nbsp;on their&nbsp;back&nbsp;in their own bed next to&nbsp;yours.
\n Safe sleep practices save lives. Provide your baby with a secure sleep environment for every nap and every night.

 Babies should only sleep in places made just for them, like a crib, cradle or bassinet. Bed sharing refers to your baby sleeping in the same bed as adults, other children, or pets. This is unsafe and there is a high risk for accidents because
 babies on adult beds can fall or get trapped between the mattress and walls or furniture
 blankets, pillows or duvets can increase the risk of suffocation
 bed sharing is even riskier if adults in the bed are under the influence of alcohol, drugs or medication that causes drowsiness
 Why room sharing is safer
 Room sharing allows your baby to sleep near you, but in their own bassinet, cradle or crib. This setup is the safest for the first six months of life.
 Many parents bring their baby into their bed for feeding or skin-to-skin contact, which is fine if the parent is awake and alert. Once feeding or snuggling is complete, place your baby on their back in their own bed next to yours.
 Safe sleep practices save lives. Provide your baby with a secure sleep environment for every nap and every night.
