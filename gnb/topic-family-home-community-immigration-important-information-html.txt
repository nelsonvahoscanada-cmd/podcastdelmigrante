 National Occupational Classification
 The National Occupational Classification (NOC) is Canada’s official system for organizing and describing jobs. Immigration New Brunswick uses it to assess your application. Choosing the correct classification code is important. It determines whether:
\n \n your work experience meets program requirements
\n a job is eligible
\n \n If the wrong code is selected, the application may be refused or delayed.
\n The second digit of each classification code represents the training, education, experience and responsibilities (TEER) level. Some pathways may have specific TEER requirements.
\n Job duties and experience must match the National Occupational Classification description, not just the job title. This ensures that applications are assessed correctly.
\n Find the classification code&nbsp; that matches the job offer and work experience.

 Reference letters
 In most cases, reference letters from current and previous employers are required. Each letter must be printed on official letterhead and include:
\n \n the candidate’s name
\n the organization’s address, telephone number, email address and website
\n the name, title and signature of their immediate supervisor or an authorized person of the organization
\n details about employment, including:
\n&nbsp; &nbsp; -&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;all positions held
\n&nbsp; &nbsp; -&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;the responsibilities and main duties of each position

\n&nbsp; &nbsp; -&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;the number of work hours per week

\n&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;-&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;they cannot be anyone who works for the paid representative who is helping with the immigration application

 Representatives
 Representatives are not required. Using one does not give priority or special treatment to the application.
\n Representatives can be paid and unpaid. When choosing a paid representative, check if they are authorized&nbsp; .
\n \n If using a representative, candidates will need to fill out the NB-007 Use of a Representative form (PDF 473 KB) 
\n Candidates who lie about using a representative may have their application refused
\n Paid representatives must be registered on the INB portal.

 Misrepresentation and fraud
 There are serious consequences for giving false or misleading information or for providing fake or altered documents.
\n If Immigration New Brunswick finds out that anyone involved in the application provided false documents or information, the application will be refused. The candidate will be banned from applying again for five years from the decision date. Additionally, the case will be reported to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).
\n"}}" id="text-7eb01548fd" class="cmp-text">
 There are serious consequences for giving false or misleading information or for providing fake or altered documents.
 If Immigration New Brunswick finds out that anyone involved in the application provided false documents or information, the application will be refused.  Maintain your status
 Those living in Canada during the application process must maintain legal status. This means you must be authorized to enter and remain in Canada as a temporary resident for a specific period.
\n Applications can be submitted to Immigration New Brunswick by a temporary resident with  maintained status&nbsp;  in Canada provided that all other program conditions are met.&nbsp;&nbsp;For example, if your program requires you to have authorization to work, you must maintain your status as a worker in Canada.
\n"}}" id="text-ea9e814a7e" class="cmp-text">
 Those living in Canada during the application process must maintain legal status. This means you must be authorized to enter and remain in Canada as a temporary resident for a specific period.
 Applications can be submitted to Immigration New Brunswick by a temporary resident with  maintained status  in Canada provided that all other program conditions are met. For example, if your program requires you to have authorization to work, you must maintain your status as a worker in Canada.
 Do not apply
 Do not apply to any of the programs if you:
\n \n are currently enrolled in post-secondary education in Canada
\n have an immigration application in process in any province or territory in Canada, including New Brunswick
\n have been refused for misrepresentation through any immigration program within the past five years
\n are not living lawfully in your current country of residence
\n have been refused admission to, or ordered to leave, Canada or any other country or territory
\n are working in Canada without authorization
\n have an active, approved or failed claim for refugee protection (asylum) or for humanitarian and compassionate consideration in Canada
