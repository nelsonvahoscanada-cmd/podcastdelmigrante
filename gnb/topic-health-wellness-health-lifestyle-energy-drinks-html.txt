 Overview
 Energy drinks contain caffeine which is known to cause various adverse effects. Caffeinated energy drinks are drinks or contain added caffeine above a specific amount. Caffeine is known to cause anxiety, nervousness, irritability, and insomnia. It can affect blood pressure and heart rate. 
\r\n Energy drinks are caffeinated beverages that may also contain:
\r\n high amounts of sugar
 taurine
 glucuronolactone
 B vitamins
 inositol
 herbs
 \r\n They are usually promoted as products that boost energy and attention.
\r\n Energy drinks are often consumed more quickly and in larger amounts than other caffeinated drinks, such as coffee and tea because they are typically:
\r\n sold in large sizes
 very high in sugar to hide the bitterness of the added caffeine, taurine and vitamins

 High caffeine content and youth
 Consumption of energy drinks by children and youth is a concern. The amount of caffeine in energy drinks varies depending on the product. Some energy drinks contain enough caffeine in one serving to exceed the daily limit for youth, with potential health risks amplified for those who consume multiple cans daily.
\r\n Health Canada advises staying below the following caffeine intake limits:
\r\n \r\n adults : 400 mg per day
\r\n children and young people under 18 years of age : 2.5 mg per kilogram of body weight

 Consumption of energy drinks by children and youth is a concern. The amount of caffeine in energy drinks varies depending on the product. Some energy drinks contain enough caffeine in one serving to exceed the daily limit for youth, with potential health risks amplified for those who consume multiple cans daily.
 Health Canada advises staying below the following caffeine intake limits:
 adults : 400 mg per day
 children and young people under 18 years of age : 2.5 mg per kilogram of body weight
 Example: a teenager who weighs 50 kg (about 110 pounds) should not consume more than 125 mg of caffeine per day.

 It may be more difficult to convince teens to limit their limit their intake of energy drinks. Talk to them about the risks to help them make an informed decision. Promote healthier alternatives instead, like water, milk and fruit infused water.

 Health risks
 Having more caffeine than recommended can have adverse effects such as:
\r\n \r\n difficulty sleeping
\r\n headaches
\r\n irritability, nervousness, anxiety, difficulty concentrating
\r\n abnormally fast heart rate
\r\n dehydration
\r\n dependence, for example: feeling like you need caffeine for function “normally”
\r\n \r\n Energy drinks usually contain a lot of sugar too. Excessive sugar consumption is associated with many health problems, such as tooth decay.
\r\n Due to the stimulant effects of caffeine, energy drinks are not recommended for:
\r\n \r\n people who are pregnant or breastfeeding
\r\n children
\r\n teenagers
\r\n people who are sensitive to caffeine
\r\n \r\n The stimulant effect of caffeine also puts you at higher risk if you have certain health conditions or are taking some types of medications (e.g., to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder or sleep disorders, etc.)&nbsp; Talk to your doctor or pharmacist for advice.

 Energy drinks and alcohol
 Energy drinks should not be mixed with alcohol. This mix can:
\r\n \r\n increase the risk of drinking more alcohol
\r\n mask the feeling of drunkenness or fatigue associated with drinking alcohol
\r\n lead to a false sense of security and an underestimation of risks
\r\n increase the adoption of problematic or risky behaviours (risky sexual behaviours, driving while impaired, etc.)
\r\n have effects on thinking, memory, and concentration.
