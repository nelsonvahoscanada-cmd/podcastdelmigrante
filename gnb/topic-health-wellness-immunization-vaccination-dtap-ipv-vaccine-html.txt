Diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and polio (DTaP-IPV) vaccine
 The DTaP-IPV vaccine protects against four diseases: diphtheria, tetanus (lockjaw), pertussis (whooping cough)&nbsp;and polio.

 The DTaP-IPV vaccine protects against four diseases: diphtheria, tetanus (lockjaw), pertussis (whooping cough) and polio.
 On this page
 About the vaccine
 The DTaP-IPV vaccine is publicly funded, making it free as a part of the routine New Brunswick Immunization Schedule for children. It is given as an injection into the upper arm.
\r\n Multiple vaccines can be administered at the same time. This means earlier protection against multiple diseases and fewer appointments. Receiving multiple vaccines at the same time does not compromise the effectiveness of each vaccine.
\r\n The vaccine is provided as a booster dose for children who have already been immunized against these diseases at a younger age. It is given as one dose at four years of age.

 Benefits of getting the vaccine
 The DTaP-IPV vaccine is the best way to protect against diphtheria, tetanus (lockjaw), pertussis (whooping cough)&nbsp;and polio.&nbsp;These&nbsp;diseases&nbsp;can cause serious illness and complications, including death.&nbsp;When your child is immunized, they help protect others as well.
\r\n"}}" id="text-7a7c1426b4" class="cmp-text">
 The DTaP-IPV vaccine is the best way to protect against diphtheria, tetanus (lockjaw), pertussis (whooping cough) and polio. These diseases can cause serious illness and complications, including death. When your child is immunized, they help protect others as well.
 Individuals who should not receive the vaccine
 The vaccine should not be given if your child&nbsp;had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past or is allergic to any of the ingredients of the vaccine or its container.
\r\n"}}" id="text-a752d63cf3" class="cmp-text">

 Vaccine reactions and side effects
 Inform your immunization provider if you or your child has any health problems or allergies.
\n Reactions are generally mild and last one to two days. The most common reactions include soreness, redness and/or swelling where the vaccine was given. Some children may experience a fever, crankiness, drowsiness or loss of appetite.&nbsp;For mild reactions, a cold compress at the injection site may reduce discomfort.  Ask a&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider what medicine is best to reduce fever.
\n With any vaccine there is an extremely rare possibility (less than one in a million people) of a life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving the vaccine. Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat, tongue or lips. This reaction can be treated. Seek immediate medical attention if you have left the vaccination site and develop serious symptoms. 
\n If your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact your primary care provider, pharmacy, Public Health office&nbsp;or Tele-Care (811).

 Booking a vaccine appointment
 In New Brunswick, the publicly funded DTaP-IPV vaccine is provided at your local Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité )&nbsp;or health-care provider.

 Records
 Vaccination records for people aged 16 and older are available by registering online at MyHealthNB . To access your immunization record, please register or sign&nbsp;in to&nbsp;your account.
