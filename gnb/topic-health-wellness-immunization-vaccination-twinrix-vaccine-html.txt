 About the vaccine
 The hepatitis A and B vaccine&nbsp;(Twinrix®)&nbsp;provides protection against both the hepatitis A and B viruses. The vaccine is given as an injection into the upper arm in older children and adults and into the thigh in infants. 
\r\n The New Brunswick Immunization Schedule provides this vaccine for individuals with health conditions that place them at greater risk of hepatitis A and B. Talk to&nbsp;a&nbsp;health-care provider to see if you are eligible.
\r\n Individuals who do not qualify&nbsp;for this publicly funded vaccine but still wish to receive it for other reasons such as traveling abroad may get it through&nbsp;a&nbsp;travel clinic at a cost. 
\r\n Multiple vaccines can be&nbsp;given&nbsp;at the same time. This means&nbsp;earlier&nbsp;protection against multiple diseases and fewer appointments. Receiving multiple vaccines at the same time&nbsp;is safe and&nbsp;does not compromise the effectiveness of each vaccine.

 Benefits of getting the vaccine
 The combined Hepatitis A and B vaccine helps protect against two highly contagious viruses that may lead to serious health problems. Getting the vaccine also helps protect others. While developing countries have the highest&nbsp;rate&nbsp;of hepatitis, the viruses are present worldwide, including in New Brunswick.

 The combined Hepatitis A and B vaccine helps protect against two highly contagious viruses that may lead to serious health problems. Getting the vaccine also helps protect others. While developing countries have the highest rate of hepatitis, the viruses are present worldwide, including in New Brunswick.
 Individuals who should not receive the vaccine
 The vaccine should not be given if you or your child had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past or is allergic to any ingredient&nbsp;in&nbsp;the vaccine or its container. 
\r\n"}}" id="text-2399467f24" class="cmp-text">
 The vaccine should not be given if you or your child had a severe allergic reaction to the vaccine in the past or is allergic to any ingredient in the vaccine or its container. 
 Vaccine reactions and side effects
 Inform your immunization provider if you or your child has any health problems or allergies.  
\n The vaccine is well tolerated. Reactions are generally mild and limited to soreness and redness at the injection site. Other less common reactions include a slight fever or feeling tired for a day or two. &nbsp;For mild reactions, a cold compress at the injection site may reduce discomfort. &nbsp;Ask a&nbsp;health-care&nbsp;provider what medicine is&nbsp;best to&nbsp;reduce fever.
\n With any vaccine, there is an extremely rare possibility (less than one in a million people) of a life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving the vaccine. Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat, tongue or lips. This reaction can be treated.  Seek immediate medical attention if you have left the vaccination site and develop serious symptoms. 
\n If you or your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact your primary care provider, pharmacy,&nbsp;Public Health office&nbsp;or Tele-Care (811).

For this reason, it is important to remain in the clinic for 15 minutes after receiving the vaccine. Signs of this reaction may include rash, difficulty breathing or swelling of the throat, tongue or lips.  If you or your child experiences unusual reactions to the vaccine or you have any questions or concerns, please contact your primary care provider, pharmacy, Public Health office or Tele-Care (811). 
 Booking a vaccine appointment
 Individuals who qualify for the vaccine can receive it at local Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité ) or&nbsp;through&nbsp;a&nbsp;primary care provider.
\n Those who would like the vaccine for travel purposes can receive it at a cost&nbsp;through&nbsp;a travel clinic or local pharmacy.

 Individuals who qualify for the vaccine can receive it at local Public Health office ( Horizon , Vitalité ) or through a primary care provider.
 Those who would like the vaccine for travel purposes can receive it at a cost through a travel clinic or local pharmacy.
 Records
 Vaccination records for people aged 16 and older are available by registering online at MyHealthNB . To access your immunization record, please register or sign in to your account. If your vaccination record is not available at MyHealthNB , contact the place you received your vaccines.  
\r\n The paper record of immunization provided when vaccinated will continue to be the official record. Keep your immunization record in a safe place.  
\r\n If the information on your record is incorrect or you received immunizations outside of New Brunswick and want them added, you can send an email to immunizationrecordupdates@gnb.ca .
