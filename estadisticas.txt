Key numbers on Canadian immigration

Understanding permanent residence numbers in Canada

Data as of August 31, 2026

Through the Immigration Levels Plan, we’re making our immigration system more responsive to the needs of the country.

The 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan is now available

On November 5, 2026, we released Canada’s 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan. The data below reflects progress toward the targets and commitments included in the 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan.

We have committed to returning immigration to sustainable levels, stabilizing the number of new permanent residents each year to less than 1% of our total population beyond 2027 with a focus on giving permanent residence to people already living in, working in, and contributing to our country.

The data below provides a snapshot of how many people have received permanent resident status in Canada this year.

The data is approximate and subject to change.

About the data
Permanent residence in 2026 under the Immigration Levels Plan

Each year, we aim to meet the number of new permanent residents (PR) set out in the Immigration Levels Plan. The plan outlines the total number of available permanent resident spaces, and how they are divided across immigration categories. This gives us flexibility to adjust to evolving priorities and pressures and stay responsive.

In recent years, about half of new permanent residents were already living in Canada as non-permanent residents, and contributing to our economy. This helps to limit additional pressures on infrastructure and social services.

Increasing French-speaking permanent residents outside Quebec

In 2026, we’re aiming for 9% (30,150) of our new permanent residents outside Quebec to be francophone or bilingual. This goal is part of the levels plan, and it aligns with the Policy on Francophone Immigration—ensuring it’s both ambitious and achievable.

Refugees and protected persons

Refugees identified for resettlement abroad are referred by the United Nations Refugee Agency, private sponsors and other referral partners. Resettled refugees arrive in Canada as permanent residents. Protected persons in Canada are people who have been granted protection by the Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada or through IRCC’s pre-removal risk assessment process. Protected persons can then apply to become permanent residents.

One-time initiatives for 2026 and 2027

As part of our commitment to recalibrate the immigration system and reduce our temporary population to less than 5% of Canada’s total population, we’ve introduced two one-time initiatives that will run in 2026 and 2027. These permanent residents are in addition to, not part of, the targets set in the 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan.

Accelerating permanent residence for skilled workers

The one-time In-Canada Workers Initiative is accelerating permanent residence for 33,000 workers who are already contributing to Canadian communities in in-demand sectors, with a particular focus on those living in smaller communities.

In 2026, we plan to admit at least 20,000 workers as permanent residents through this initiative. The remaining will be admitted in 2027.

Offering stability to people with protected status

This one-time, two-year initiative will process permanent residence applications from up to 115,000 eligible protected persons and their in-Canada dependants. Protected persons have recognized protected status in Canada and can’t return safely to their country of origin.

In 2026, we intend to grant permanent residence to 46,000 protected persons and their in-Canada dependants through this initiative.

Total number of new permanent residents in Canada

The number of people becoming permanent residents each year is stabilizing under our Immigration Levels Plan to align immigration with available services and infrastructure.

In addition, processing additional permanent residence applications under our two one-time initiatives (OTIs) will recalibrate the immigration system and provide stability to people who are already in Canada contributing to our economy. Combining the numbers from the levels plan and these initiatives gives a full view of how many people become permanent residents each year.
