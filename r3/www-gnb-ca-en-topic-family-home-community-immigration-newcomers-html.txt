 Housing
 Newcomers in New Brunswick can choose to rent or buy a home, depending on their needs, plans and status in Canada. When you’re looking for a home to buy or rent, you’ll want to think about the location. If you are not yet familiar with New Brunswick, start by exploring our cities and regions .
 Renting a home
 Renting a home in New Brunswick may be quite different from what you are used to. It is important to learn as much as you can before you sign your lease. Learn more about renting in New Brunswick , including your rights and responsibilities.
 Buying a home
 Once you are ready to settle in long term, you may be looking for a home to purchase and call your own. Learn about buying a home in Canada , including how to work with a real estate agent, get financing and get mortgage insurance.
 Additional resources
 Housing for Newcomers to Canada : a guide from the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) explains renting, buying and owning a home in Canada
 Housing in Canada - gov.can : immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) has information on housing for newcomers
 Health care
 In New Brunswick, primary health services are covered by the provincial government. Eligible individuals can access these services through Medicare, a tax-funded health insurance plan. The provincial government determines which services are covered by Medicare.
 Services that aren’t included, such as prescription drugs, dental care and vision care, may be covered by private insurance, which can be purchased individually or offered by an employer as part of a benefits package. As a newcomer, one of your first tasks is to get a Medicare card.
 Apply for Medicare coverage 
 For more information on services and accessing health care, visit health and wellness . 
 Children and families
 Child care
 In New Brunswick, the law requires that children under the age of 12 must always be supervised. There are many early learning and child care facilities across New Brunswick. They provide structured, supervised care for children. Programs often include age-appropriate activities that support learning and development.
 Education
 As Canada’s only officially bilingual province, New Brunswick offers education through two distinct sectors: Anglophone and Francophone. In each system, schools will teach and communicate in the language of their respective system.
 For more information, visit education and training .
