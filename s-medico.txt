 Longitudinal care 
 As a resident of Whitehorse or a surrounding area, you can register to be matched with a primary care provider who’s accepting new patients. You can also learn about how ongoing care works in the Yukon.
 Register for a primary care provider 
 Eligibility
 To use this service, you must:
 have a valid Yukon Health Care Insurance Plan (YHCIP);
 be 16 years old or older;
 not have a family doctor or nurse practitioner; and
 live in the Whitehorse catchment area.
 Catchment area
 The Whitehorse catchment area includes addresses:
 in the City of Whitehorse;
 on the North Alaska Highway up to Champagne Landing;
 on the South Alaska Highway up to Jake's Corner;
 on the North Klondike Highway up to Braeburn; and
 on the South Klondike Highway up to Annie Lake Road.
 This includes Marsh Lake, Mount Lorne, Ibex Valley, Mendenhall, Champagne Landing, Golden Horn, Braeburn, Pilot Mountain and other addresses within these boundaries.
 [https://yukon.ca/en/health-and-wellness/care-services/find-hospital-or-health-centre] Find a hospital or community health centre outside the Whitehorse area. 
 You can access [https://yukon.ca/en/health-and-wellness/care-services/find-hospital-or-health-centre] sexual or reproductive health support and menopause health support in Whitehorse without a referral from a health care provider.
 Enrolling others
 Adults can enrol their partners in the program. Parents or guardians can enrol their dependents under the age of 19. Your partner or dependents must live at the same address as you. 
 Register together to be matched with the same family doctor or nurse practitioner. 
 Apply online
 Complete the online form
 [https://ihs.gov.yk.ca/find-a-family-doctor/f?p=100:1] Find a primary care provider 
 Apply in person
 Download the [https://yukon.ca/en/find-primary-care-provider-printable-form] Find a primary care provider form .
 Fill out and sign the form.
 Submit the form in person:
 Government of Yukon
Insured Health Services
204 Lambert Street, 4th floor
Whitehorse, Yukon.
 Find a licensed professional 
 We maintain online public registers so you can confirm whether a regulated professional or company has an active Yukon licence.
 [https://ykplra.in1touch.org/site/registry] Search the Yukon online registry 
 If you would like to know if a physician holds a Yukon licence, visit the [https://www.yukonmedicalcouncil.ca/] Yukon Medical Council’s website .
 If you would like to know if a registered nurse holds a Yukon licence, you can contact the [/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection#4627222b2f28063f342827682527] Yukon Registered Nurses Association .
 Longitudinal care 
 Longitudinal care provides continuous, relationship-based care between a patient and health care provider over time. We created the Longitudinal Family Medicine Program to reinforce continuity of care, accountability and strengthen patient access to care.
 Read the [https://yukon.ca/en/memorandum-agreement-yukon-medical-association] Memorandum of Agreement between the Government of Yukon and the Yukon Medical Association .
 If your family doctor is part of this program, you may benefit from:
 greater access to same-day appointments for urgent, non-emergency needs;
 more consistent, ongoing care from your primary care provider; and
 improved coordination of your care over time.
 Physicians in the program are required to:
 offer and advertise at least 2 same-day appointments for every day they’re in clinic;
 accept at least 10 new patients each year from the [https://ihs.gov.yk.ca/find-a-family-doctor/f?p=100:1] Find a Primary Care Provider list;
 maintain at least 500 patients; and
 provide ongoing, relationship-based care.
